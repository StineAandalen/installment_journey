# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** The consumer - > Mara
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To agree to a plan she can afford and keep, covering both claims, without making her situation worse.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** After a reminder she opens the portal and can't tell whether a plan would be affordable, why her income is requested, or what happens if she misses a payment. If she's then asked for documents she can't easily find, she gives up or calls an agent.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Phone call to an agent iHypothesis: her banking app or email (to hunt for proof of income) and her phone's camera or photo gallery (to upload it). Nothing in the brief confirms this.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** 1. She opens the portal on her phone after a digital reminder and starts the instalment journey.
2. She reaches the income questions and is asked for documents, without being told why.
3. Hypothesis: she searches her bank app or inbox for something that proves her variable income, and may upload a poor photo or estimate a figure..
4. She stops, unsure whether a wrong answer could make things worse, and closes the portal.
5. She calls an agent, who repeats identity, affordability and case checks from scratch.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The point where she's asked for income documents she can't easily find, with no explanation, while worrying that a wrong figure could leave her worse off. Her uncertainty peaks there, and it's the moment she leaves the self-service path.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** The closest documented behaviour is the brief's description of Mara: if the journey confuses her or asks for documents she can't easily find, she calls an agent. Supporting signals, all fictional:

28% of uploaded income documents need clarification or review.
31% of journey starters contact an agent within 7 days.
54% of starters don't reach an agreement.
Early research themes 2 and 3: consumers don't understand why information is requested, and they fear the consequences of a wrong answer.
