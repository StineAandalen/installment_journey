Slide 5 · Strategy · Modules 1 + 3

Problem, value, hypothesis

Problem statement as the hook

As long as consumers can't figure out on the portal which payment plan they can stick to, they either abandon the process or agree to plans that fail—and Riverty pays the price with phone calls, more than half of its administrative workload, and a portfolio value that is masked by a rising self-service rate.

Value proposition

Before she confirms, Mara sees a preselected instalment below the maximum and one sentence that names the income already stored. She can agree to a plan she can keep. Every further month counts agreements as a success even though 19% miss a payment within 60 days, while 31% of starters call within 7 days.

Data-backed hypothesis

I expect Affordability guidance, not just a maximum, to lead Mara to see a comfortable instalment and one sentence about the income already stored before she decides, measured by a drop of at least 5 percentage points in the share of starters who contact an agent within 7 days, from 31% to 26% or lower, over 14 days of enrollment plus 7 days of follow-up. We will protect the share of new agreements kept through the first 60 days, which must stay at or above 81%, for the whole test.

Workaround and the journey

Competitive analysis / workaround

A phone call with the collections agent, a letter sent alongside the digital reminder, and proof of income that she has to find and upload outside the portal.

  • She searches Google to find out what happens if she misses a payment, why she’s asked about her income, and whether the two requirements go hand in hand.
  • She calculates her ability to pay on her own—using her bank account, notes, or a spreadsheet.
  • She looks for proof of income, which she can’t find at that moment, so she either gives up or calls.

“If the process confuses her or requires documents she can’t easily find, she calls a representative.”

22% of portal visitors start. 31% of starters call within 7 days. 28% of uploaded proofs still need clarification.

Who and what breaks

Mara, stable income that fluctuates with shift work, one or more open claims, little time. She wants a plan she can keep without making her situation worse.

The moment on her phone when the process requires documents and the crucial questions remain unanswered. She cannot tell whether the plan will make her situation worse, so she gives up or calls.

PRD highlights · Affordability guidance, not just a maximum

Must

  • The preselected instalment is below the maximum and is marked as comfortable.
  • One sentence of reasoning, tied to the income figure she already gave in this journey.
  • Confirmation stays closed until both the comfortable instalment and the sentence are rendered, and repeats the selected monthly amount.
  • The maximum is visible and is not preselected.

Should

  • A lower and a higher option beside the suggestion. The higher one is not preselected.
  • The monthly amount and the term in months appear in the same view.

Won’t now

  • A rate set by a model with no readable rule.
  • The highest permitted instalment as the default.
  • A new income range, proof of income, combining both claims, missed-payment consequences, or reminders on this step.
  • No income stored: show no instalment and no reason. If only the maximum is permitted, do not label it comfortable and do not preselect it. Never invent an income figure or a rate.

Slide 8 · Validation · Module 5

Experiment plan

There is no result yet. The test is planned, not run. Do not ship on day 21.

Hypothesis

Affordability guidance leads Mara to see a comfortable instalment and one sentence about the income already stored before she decides. The share of starters who contact an agent within 7 days falls by at least 5 percentage points, from 31% to 26% or lower, with a two-sided p-value below 0.05. The share of new agreements kept through the first 60 days stays at or above 81%.

Control A

The unchanged plan step does not preselect a comfortable instalment, does not mark one, and gives no reason tied to the income already stored. Same portal, stored income, and maximum.

Variant B

One comfortable instalment is preselected and marked, strictly below the maximum. The maximum is shown and is not selected. One sentence names the income already stored. Confirmation opens only when both are on screen, and repeats the monthly amount.

Primary
31%

Starters who call within 7 days. Counts the call only.

MDE
5 pp

31% to 26%. 1,398 starters per arm. 50/50. p < 0.05, two-sided.

Duration
14+7

Days of enrollment plus follow-up. Extend enrollment if needed. Do not lower the MDE.

Guardrail
81%

Kept through 60 days. Stop if the miss rate in an arm rises above 19%. Highest instalment preselected in 0%.

Ship

Only if the 7-day contact rate in B is at least 5 percentage points below A, p is under 0.05, and 60 days later the keep rate in B is at least 81%. The highest instalment was preselected in 0% of B.

Iterate

If the call rate falls but by less than 5 percentage points, or the direction is right and p is 0.05 or higher.

Stop

If the call rate does not fall or rises, or if the keep rate in B falls below 81%, even when calls fell.

Slide 10 · Story

Friction, the click, and what is next

Friction

The hard part was cutting four open questions down to one feature. Affordability, the reason for the income question, a missed payment, and the two claims all sat in the same moment. V1 keeps only the preselected instalment and one sentence about the stored income.

Surprise

The surprise was the self-service rate. It counts the plan as a success the moment it is created. It can rise while 19% of new agreements miss a payment within 60 days.

Aha moment

The click was the test duration. The call rate can be read after 21 days. Whether Mara keeps the plan can be read only after 60 days. Fewer calls are not yet proof that she understood the instalment or can keep it. That is why there is no ship decision on day 21.

Key takeaways / what I would do next

The leading metric measures only whether starters call within 7 days. It does not measure dropout without a call. The 60-day keep rate is a different number, and it protects against counting uncertain agreements as a success. Variant B changes two things at once, the instalment and the sentence, plus the gate before confirmation. A drop in calls cannot then be attributed to one of those parts. Next I would measure, thinly, what the portal cannot show today: contact within 7 days and keeping the plan for 60 days. After that come the answers V1 still leaves open: why income is asked, what a missed payment triggers, and which document is enough. The test has not run yet. Ship only when the call rate, the p-value, the keep rate, and the rule on the maximum all hold together.
